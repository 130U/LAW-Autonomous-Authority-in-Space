# Where this Article sits

Three bodies of work bear on autonomy in space, and none of them answers the Article's question. Space law says
who is responsible and supervised, and prescribes no control architecture. The literature on meaningful human
control says when a human can still matter, mostly for weapons. Risk-risk analysis says that controls have risks
of their own. The Article joins them with a fourth, the law of delegation, and reads the two thresholds and the
timing test as what that law already asks of human delegates. This page records what each body of authority
settles, what it leaves open, and what the Article adds. The complete list of 159 authorities and sources is in
the [bibliography](bibliography.md).

| Weight | Sources | What they settle | What they leave open |

| **Binding domestic law (United States)** | 47 C.F.R. § 25.114(d)(14); 14 C.F.R. § 450.108; *Carter Coal*, *State Farm*, *U.S. Telecom*, *Loper Bright*, *New York Times Co. v. ## What the Article adds

| Body of work | Where it stops | The Article's step |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Space-law scholarship on AI | It asks who is responsible for an autonomous system | It asks who holds which decision right at which stage, and separates authority from evidence |
| Meaningful human control | It defines control for weapons and treats timing as an ethical condition | It makes timing a legal test, the point at which review can still change the outcome, and adds advance-constrained action and justified fallback as distinct supervisory choices |
| Risk-risk analysis | It compares a technology with its alternatives | It applies the comparison to the controls themselves, and counts the benefits of regulation as well as its costs |
| The law of delegation | It governs delegation among human institutions | It supplies the pedigree of the procedure: no private power over rivals, a justification on the record, and a final decision exercised in time |

## Key authorities

### Treaty law and its interpretation

<a id="ost1967"></a>
**Treaty on Principles Governing the Activities of States in the Exploration and Use of Outer Space, arts. VI to IX, Jan. 27, 1967, 610 U.N.T.S. 205.** [text](https://www.nasa.gov/history/SP-4225/documentation/cooperation/treaty.htm)
Article VI makes states responsible for national activities and requires authorization and continuing supervision.
Article VIII keeps registry jurisdiction and ownership, and Article IX requires due regard and consultation. The
treaty governs the actors and prescribes no cadence of approval, which is the space the Article's procedure fills.

<a id="liability1972"></a>
**Convention on International Liability for Damage Caused by Space Objects, Mar. 29, 1972, 961 U.N.T.S. 187.** [text](https://www.unoosa.org/oosa/en/ourwork/spacelaw/treaties/liability-convention.html)
Absolute liability on Earth and to aircraft, fault liability in space, and claims through states. Fault is left
undefined. The Article shows that an authorization record supplies the account of available precautions a fault
standard needs.

<a id="itlos2011"></a>
**Responsibilities and Obligations of States Sponsoring Persons and Entities with Respect to Activities in the Area, Case No. 17, Advisory Opinion of Feb. 1, 2011, 2011 ITLOS Rep. 10.** [case page](https://www.itlos.org/en/main/cases/list-of-cases/case-no-17/)
A sponsoring state's obligation to ensure a contractor's compliance is one of conduct and of due diligence, and
due diligence is "a variable concept" that changes with knowledge and with the risks of the activity. The opinion
concerns the deep seabed. Its account of due diligence is the best available reading of what continuing
supervision requires when the facts move.

<a id="ilc2001"></a>
**International Law Commission, Draft Articles on Prevention of Transboundary Harm from Hazardous Activities (2001).** [text](https://legal.un.org/ilc/texts/instruments/english/commentaries/9_7_2001.pdf)
Prior authorization for a hazardous activity, fresh authorization for any major change, and termination where the
terms are not met. A revisable authorization with reassessment triggers is therefore an established form, and the
Article carries it to delegated execution.

### United States law

<a id="statefarm"></a>
**Motor Vehicle Mfrs. Ass'n of the U.S., Inc. v. State Farm Mut. Auto. Ins. Co., 463 U.S. 29 (1983).**
An agency must examine the relevant data and articulate a satisfactory explanation, and it acts arbitrarily when
it fails to consider an important aspect of the problem. The Article's second threshold is this requirement,
applied to the decision to delegate.

<a id="cartercoal"></a>
**Carter v. Carter Coal Co., 298 U.S. 238 (1936).**
A statute that let one group of producers set terms for their competitors was delegation "in its most obnoxious
form". An operator whose maneuver policy moves risk onto a neighboring operator exercises power of that kind, and
a better predictor does not answer the objection. This is the first threshold.

<a id="faa450"></a>
**14 C.F.R. § 450.108 (flight abort).** [eCFR](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-14/chapter-III/subchapter-C/part-450/subpart-C/section-450.108)
A licensed launch operator sets flight safety limits in advance, and a flight need not be aborted where abort
would not materially decrease the risk from a high-consequence event. A binding rule already delegates a drastic
action to pre-set limits and treats the fallback as a choice with risks of its own.

<a id="fcc2020"></a>
**Mitigation of Orbital Debris in the New Space Age, Report and Order, 85 Fed. Reg. 52,422 (Aug. 25, 2020), and Order on Reconsideration, 89 Fed. Reg. 13,276 (Feb. 22, 2024).** [2020](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2020-08-25/html/2020-13185.htm) · [2024](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2024-02-22/html/2024-03506.htm)
Applicants describe maneuverability and certify that they will assess and mitigate conjunction risk. The orders
show operational detail entering through a licensing regime, and they address no rule to autonomy as such.

<a id="loperbright"></a>
**Loper Bright Enters. v. Raimondo, 603 U.S. 369 (2024).** [opinion](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/23pdf/22-451_7m58.pdf)
A reviewing court decides for itself whether an agency has acted within its statutory authority. A condition on
autonomous maneuvering would be tested against the text of the Communications Act, which makes the choice of
institution a first-order question for any proposal of this kind.

### State practice

<a id="notes2021"></a>
**Note Verbale Dated 3 December 2021 from the Permanent Mission of China, U.N. Doc. A/AC.105/1262; Note Verbale Dated 28 January 2022 from the Permanent Mission of the United States, U.N. Doc. A/AC.105/1265.** [A/AC.105/1262](https://documents.un.org/api/symbol/access?s=A/AC.105/1262&l=en&t=pdf)
China reported that its space station twice maneuvered to avoid Starlink satellites, one of them continuously
maneuvering with a strategy that was unknown, and quoted Article VI. The United States replied that the encounters
did not meet its emergency collision criteria. The exchange is the clearest state practice on the question the
authorization record is meant to answer in advance: whose threshold governs.

### Human control

<a id="santonidesio2018"></a>
**Filippo Santoni de Sio & Jeroen van den Hoven, *Meaningful Human Control over Autonomous Systems: A Philosophical Account*, 5 Frontiers Robotics & AI 15 (2018).** [text](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/robotics-and-ai/articles/10.3389/frobt.2018.00015/full)
Meaningful control is defined by tracking and tracing conditions, and being in the loop is neither sufficient nor
necessary. The account separates the time of an action from the time accountable humans shape it. It is a
philosophical account; the Article gives the separation a legal form.

<a id="ccw2019"></a>
**Group of Governmental Experts on Lethal Autonomous Weapons Systems, Report of the 2019 Session, U.N. Doc. CCW/GGE.1/2019/3.** [text](https://docs-library.unoda.org/Convention_on_Certain_Conventional_Weapons_-_Group_of_Governmental_Experts_(2019)/1919338E.pdf)
States recorded that human responsibility can be exercised at different stages of a system's life cycle. The
timing test has an antecedent in that process. The weapons debate does not address civil operators who impose
risks on one another.

<a id="green2022"></a>
**Ben Green, *The Flaws of Policies Requiring Human Oversight of Government Algorithms*, 45 Computer L. & Security Rev. 105681 (2022).** [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.05067)
People generally cannot perform the oversight that human-oversight policies assume, so the policies legitimize the
tools they are meant to check. The finding supports the Article's rule that individual approval has to be
justified by evidence that review can still change the outcome.

<a id="bainbridge1983"></a>
**Lisanne Bainbridge, *Ironies of Automation*, 19 Automatica 775 (1983).** [doi](https://doi.org/10.1016/0005-1098(83)90046-8)
Automation leaves operators the tasks it cannot do and erodes the skills they need for them. The Article uses
deskilling as a countervailing risk of autonomy and as a reason the value of an override has to be shown.

### Risk-risk method

<a id="felgenhauer2022"></a>
**Tyler Felgenhauer et al., Solar Radiation Modification: A Risk-Risk Analysis (Carnegie Climate Governance Initiative 2022).** [pdf](https://www.c2g2.net/wp-content/uploads/202203-C2G-RR-Full.pdf)
Each policy option is scored on benefits, costs, co-benefits, countervailing risks and distribution. The Article
takes the method and adds one requirement: the population, the baseline and the endpoint are fixed before a
quadrant is named, because the classification depends on them.

<a id="rascoff2002"></a>
**Samuel J. Rascoff & Richard L. Revesz, *The Biases of Risk Tradeoff Analysis*, 69 U. Chi. L. Rev. 1763 (2002).**
Risk-tradeoff analysis as practiced counts the harms a regulation causes and neglects its ancillary benefits. The
Article applies the correction to its own procedure: records, assurance exercises and reconstructible decisions
have benefits that go in the same ledger as delay and expense.

### The technical record

<a id="francis2024"></a>
**Raymond Francis et al., AEGIS Autonomous Targeting for the SuperCam Instrument on the Mars 2020 Perseverance Rover (i-SAIRAS 2024).** [pdf](https://superintelligence.jpl.nasa.gov/public/documents/papers/francis-isairas-2024.pdf)
Onboard target selection within parameters set by scientists, with rover flight software that independently denies
unsafe pointing. This is advance-constrained action with protections outside the task-selection process, the
arrangement the Article treats as the defensible case.

<a id="dart2006"></a>
**NASA, Overview of the DART Mishap Investigation Results (2006).** [pdf](https://www.nasa.gov/pdf/148072main_DART_mishap_overview.pdf)
An autonomous rendezvous vehicle collided with its target although its collision-avoidance function had
activated, because that function depended on the same navigation data as the guidance system. A protection that
shares the controller's picture of the world protects only against errors the controller does not make.

<a id="uriot2020"></a>
**Thomas Uriot et al., *Spacecraft Collision Avoidance Challenge: Design and Results of a Machine Learning Competition* (2020).** [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.03069v2)
Teams predicted a later risk estimate from earlier conjunction data, and a naive baseline was hard to beat. A
competition on prediction shows what prediction can do. It is silent on who may maneuver and at whose expense,
which is the Article's subject.

<a id="sheikh2021"></a>
**Sofia Z. Sheikh et al., *Analysis of the Breakthrough Listen Signal of Interest blc1 with a Technosignature Verification Framework*, 5 Nature Astronomy 1153 (2021).** [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.06350v1)
A signal that passed initial screening was traced to local interference. A candidate is not a conclusion, and
confirmation needs independent observation, which is a matter for scientific verification and not for spacecraft
licensing.
