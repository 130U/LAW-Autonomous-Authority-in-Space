# Argument map

The Article's argument Part by Part: each claim, the authority it rests on, the strongest objection to it and the
response. Authorities are cited in full in the notes of the
[Article](../manuscript/autonomous-authority-in-space.md).

## Thesis

Autonomy in space should be regulated by allocating decision rights at identifiable stages of an activity. Lawful
authority to undertake an activity is one question, and evidence that justifies delegating its execution to
software is another. Only when both are answered does the question become when human review can still change the
outcome. The two thresholds and the timing test restate, for autonomous systems, what the law of delegation already
asks of human delegates.

## Part II. From risk classification to a comparative decision procedure

| Claim | Authority | Objection | Response |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Risk-risk categories are useful only after baseline, endpoint and population are fixed | Graham and Wiener's taxonomy; Felgenhauer et al.; the worked cases of deskilling and SETI errors | The categories are a neutral classification and can be applied directly | Whether an effect is a "transfer" depends on where the population boundary is drawn, and aggregation can hide a distributional conflict |
| The baseline is a feasible workflow, and regulation is itself an object of comparison | Comparative institutional analysis | Comparing with an ideal sets a higher safety bar | An infeasible comparator distorts the choice |
| Distribution is a separate question from aggregate benefit | The legal interests of third parties who have not consented | A net-beneficial maneuver should be allowed | Rights and duties constrain how risks may be traded; a private estimate of benefit leaves them in force |
| Individual review is valuable only where it can still change the outcome | The literature on meaningful human control | Approval preserves a chain of command | That benefit is weighed against delay, and accountability can be preserved upstream |
| Two thresholds, then timing: authority, evidence, and the point at which review still matters | The above | "No time to ask" justifies autonomy | It justifies advance-constrained action or a justified fallback, and never unrestricted autonomy |
| Each element of the procedure has a counterpart in the law of delegation | *Carter v. Carter Coal Co.* (private power over rivals); *State Farm* (a decision justified on a record); *U.S. Telecom Ass'n v. FCC* (subdelegation with the final decision retained) | Delegation doctrine concerns human institutions | A final decision that arrives after the action is complete is kept in name only. The principal's judgment has to be exercised earlier and embodied in bounded terms, which is what advance authorization of an action class does |

## Part III. Existing law governs the actors but leaves operational choices

| Claim | Authority | Objection | Response |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Continuing supervision under Article VI applies when software executes commands | Outer Space Treaty arts. VI to IX | The text has no "algorithm", so autonomy is outside supervision | Supervision applies throughout an activity; advance authorization serves it only if the state can learn of material changes and act on them |
| Article VI is a special rule and does not depend on attributing each private command | ILC Articles on State Responsibility arts. 2, 4, 8, 55 | Private autonomous acts escape state responsibility | Article VI covers nongovernmental national activities expressly |
| Continuing supervision is a duty that moves with the facts | ITLOS Seabed Disputes Chamber, Advisory Opinion (2011), ¶¶ 110, 117; ILC Prevention Articles art. 6 | Authorization is granted once | Due diligence is "a variable concept" that changes with knowledge and risk, and the prevention articles require fresh authorization for any major change. Reassessment triggers are what due diligence ordinarily requires |
| Due regard and consultation give no veto and no numerical threshold | Outer Space Treaty art. IX | Article IX already sets a collision standard | It requires consultation in specified conditions; the authorization record gives it practical effect |
| Liability supplies incentives and incomplete prevention | Liability Convention arts. I to III, VI to XII, XIX | Liability alone is enough | Claims run through states, awards may be recommendatory, and diffuse losses may fall outside "damage" |
| Autonomy complicates proof of fault without removing the actor | Liability Convention art. III | A machine is not a legal person, so there is no fault | Fault can lie in design, delegation, maintenance or use |
| Registration is not an operational record, and the sustainability guidelines are voluntary | Registration Convention; COPUOS Long-term Sustainability Guidelines | There is a normative vacuum | There is a coordination problem with voluntary but real norms |
| Operational detail enters through domestic institutions with statutory limits | 47 C.F.R. § 25.114(d)(14); the FCC's 2020 and 2024 orbital-debris orders | The FCC can regulate autonomy generally | Article VI confers no power on the FCC; a new condition has to fit the Communications Act |
| The choice of institution is a first-order question | *Loper Bright Enterprises v. Raimondo* | Agencies receive deference on ambiguous statutes | Since 2024 a court decides for itself whether the agency acted within its authority, so a condition on autonomous maneuvering would be tested against the statute's text |

## Part IV. Delayed spacecraft operations and the case for advance permission

| Claim | Authority | Objection | Response |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Latency changes the comparator and leaves ground expertise useful | NASA analysis of communication delays to Mars | Delay proves software must manage emergencies | It proves that some responses must be local |
| Flight evidence supports a narrow claim | Remote Agent; AEGIS on Curiosity and Perseverance | Success shows autonomy is safe | It shows task-specific action under safeguards set in advance; each new application needs its own evidence |
| Engineering requirements are the baseline for the added component | NASA NPR 7150.2D | Compliance certifies the component | Compliance is the baseline against which the added hazards are assessed |
| Advance permission is defensible with credible boundaries and a justified fallback | The comparison of three arrangements | Protective limits sacrifice observations | The sacrifice has to be justified, and priorities can differ by mission |
| A fallback rule of this form is already in force | 14 C.F.R. § 450.108 (flight abort) | Launch is a different activity | The regulation delegates a drastic action to limits set in advance and treats the fallback as a choice whose risks are compared with those of continuing |

## Part V. Orbital safety and risks imposed on other operators

| Claim | Authority | Objection | Response |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A better prediction is one part of avoidance | The ESA collision-avoidance challenge, whose target was a later risk estimate | The best predictor should maneuver | Selecting and executing maneuvers among many operators is a separate question |
| Avoidance can redistribute exposure | The two-operator mechanism | Each operator protecting itself is safe overall | Transfers and new pathways are evaluated one by one |
| States already dispute whose threshold governs | Notes verbales of China (A/AC.105/1262) and the United States (A/AC.105/1265) | The episode was a disagreement about facts | The two states did not disagree about physics. They disagreed about whose threshold governed and what one operator is owed concerning another's maneuver policy, the questions an authorization record answers in advance |
| The maneuver policy needs justification, and the predictor is only part of it | Assurance exercises: misleading alerts, stale estimates, simultaneous maneuvers, lost channels | Urgency is a waiver | Urgency an operator created is no waiver; the emergency policy is itself justified |
| Individual constraints do not establish collective safety | Studies of run-time assurance for multiple spacecraft | Each satellite's filter keeps it safe | Licenses have to consider interactions, and a mandated common model could concentrate a shared error |
| A common protocol comes before any common model | COLREGs rules 16 and 17; airborne collision avoidance and the Überlingen collision | Better models would make protocols unnecessary | Sea and air traffic assign who moves and how intentions are signaled, and leave prediction to each participant. Orbital operations have the predictors and lack the protocol |
| Capture changes both the physical act and the legal interest | RemoveDEBRIS; Outer Space Treaty art. VIII | "Debris" has no owner | Ownership and registry interests persist; target and plan are authorized separately from execution |

## Part VI. SETI and the difference between a candidate and a conclusion

| Claim | Authority | Objection | Response |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Machine learning can prioritize signals and cannot interpret unknown communication | Whale-call research; the 820-star search | Classifier accuracy measures the probability of extraterrestrial origin | Test performance concerns the task the classifier was trained on |
| Candidates need verification | The signal blc1, attributed to interference | Errors would cause diplomatic escalation | That claim needs its own evidence about institutional response |
| The process is staged: candidates circulate, confirmation claims are verified | IAA Declaration of Principles (2010) | Governments should clear announcements in advance | Supervisory power over space activities does not reach terrestrial scientific speech |
| In the United States the limit is constitutional | *New York Times Co. v. United States* | Licensing powers could be extended | Advance clearance of an announcement would be a prior restraint |
| Responding is a separate act | The separation of detecting, interpreting, reporting and transmitting | Permission to search implies permission to respond | A classifier's success is no evidence about the effects of a response |

## Part VII. Implementing authority that can be revised

| Element | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Authorization record | Actor, action class, operating conditions, exposed interests, alternatives, reassessment triggers, and behavior when the authorization no longer fits ([template](proposal.md)) |
| Evidentiary burdens | The applicant shows why existing arrangements are insufficient and what the capability can do. The regulator shows why its conditions address material risks and why lighter precautions are inadequate |
| Independent scrutiny | Independent enough to challenge the baseline and the excluded scenarios, scaled to the activity, with shared test facilities for small operators |
| Event records | Observations and uncertainty, software and policy versions, the permitted range, the selected action, interventions and information exchanged |
| The record and liability | A documented operating range supplies the account of available precautions that a fault standard requires. Departure from the range is evidence of fault and operation within it is evidence of care, as with a safety standard in the common law of torts (Restatement (Third) of Torts §§ 13, 16) |
| Institutions | The FCC for covered satellites, NASA for its missions, COPUOS for interoperable practice, and new agreements only where they are needed |

## Part VIII. Objections and the risks of regulation

| Objection | Response |
| --- | --- |
| **A. The authorization may conceal the real political choice** | The record names excluded and uncertain losses and stays revisable; compliance confers no immunity; reviewers can challenge scale and objective; some activities are constrained outright |
| **B. Regulators may make safe action slower and more expensive** | Requirements are compared with lighter precautions; evidence is reusable, tests are shared, and equivalent review is recognized. The comparison runs in both directions: records support later claims and investigations, assurance exercises train operators, and a decision that can be reconstructed deters carelessness (Rascoff and Revesz) |
| **C. A validated boundary may fail outside its assumptions** | Ranges rest on evidence and are not guarantees. In NASA's 2005 DART mission the collision-avoidance logic depended on the same navigation data as the guidance system. Independence is assessed against real failure paths |
| **D. Liability alone or global control might be preferable** | Liability works when harms are compensable and provable; central control needs information, legitimacy and capacity it cannot assume |

## Conclusion

Responsibility attaches to the decisions that grant and shape authority. Delayed operations justify advance
permission for validated local action. Orbital avoidance adds coordination. Capture adds legal interests that
prediction cannot resolve. SETI separates searching, confirming, communicating and responding. The procedure asks
of an autonomous system what the law already asks of any delegate: authority first, a justification on the record,
and control exercised while it can still matter.
